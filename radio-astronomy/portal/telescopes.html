Why we moved

Why we moved to a steerable telescope

  1. Fixed telescopesPSU + PNU
  2. Experience and limits of fixed drift-scan observingWhat we learned
  3. PSU moving telescopeDISH222
  4. Active research platformToday

The fixed telescopes are 5 m reflectors pointing straight up. As the Earth turns, a strip of sky at the station's declination drifts through the beam, so each fixed telescope maps one band of sky per day. The design is simple and robust, and it was published in IEEE Antennas and Propagation Magazine (2025).

But a fixed telescope cannot choose where to look, repeat an observation on demand, or point at a calibration source. To map chosen regions of the Galactic plane, the project moved to a motorised dish that steers in azimuth and elevation: DISH222, now the active research telescope.
