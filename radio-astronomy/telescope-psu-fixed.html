PSU fixed telescope: the drift-scan origin of the project

A fixed reflector that looks straight up and lets the Earth do the scanning. This is where the project's historical data begin.

Instrument

A fixed 5 m reflector pointing at the zenith

The project's first telescope generation, and the historical entry point to the whole data story.

Reflector
5 m parabolic reflector with approximately f/D = 0.35.
Feed
Choke-horn/feed and low-noise receiving chain.
Performance
Approximately 35 dBi gain and approximately 2.5° beamwidth.
Method
Fixed zenith transit-scan observations using Earth rotation.

Values as reported for the published fixed implementation (IEEE Antennas and Propagation Magazine, 2025). Station-specific details for PSU are to be added from the station record.

Zenith-pointing operation

Available (summary)

The reflector points straight up and never moves. The beam therefore stays on one band of sky, at the declination set by the site's latitude, while the Earth turns.

Drift-scan observing

Available (summary)

As the Earth rotates, the sky drifts through the beam: each fixed telescope maps one band of sky per day. Simple and robust, but the telescope cannot choose where to look.

Limitations that motivated the moving telescope

Available (summary)

A fixed telescope cannot repeat an observation on demand, map a chosen region, or point at a calibration source. These limits led to the steerable DISH222.
